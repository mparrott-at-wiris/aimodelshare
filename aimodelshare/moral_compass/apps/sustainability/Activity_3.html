🤖 What is AI, Anyway?

In Activity 2, you identified Site EUI as the key metric and selected features like floor area, weather data, and elevation. Now let's understand how AI uses those features to make predictions.

Before you can build AI systems for climate action, you need to understand what AI actually is. Don't worry - we'll explain it in simple, everyday terms!

🎯 A Simple Definition

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is just a fancy name for:

A system that makes predictions based on patterns

That's it! Let's break down what that means...

Think About How YOU Make Predictions:

  • Weather: Dark clouds → You predict rain → You bring an umbrella
  • Traffic: Rush hour time → You predict congestion → You leave early
  • Movies: Actor you like → You predict you'll enjoy it → You watch it

AI does the same thing, but using data and math instead of human experience and intuition.

📐 The Three-Part Formula

Every AI system works the same way, following this simple formula:

1️⃣ INPUT

Data goes in

→

2️⃣ MODEL

AI processes it

→

3️⃣ OUTPUT

Prediction comes out

Real-World Examples:

INPUT: Photo of a dog
MODEL: Image recognition AI
OUTPUT: "This is a Golden Retriever"

INPUT: "How's the weather?"
MODEL: Language AI (like ChatGPT)
OUTPUT: A helpful response

INPUT: Building energy data
MODEL: Energy efficiency AI
OUTPUT: "High Efficiency" or "Low Efficiency"

🧠 How Does an AI Model Learn?

1. It Learns from Examples

An AI model isn't programmed with answers. Instead, it's trained on a huge number of examples, and it learns how to find the answers on its own.

In our climate scenario, this means feeding the model thousands of past building records (examples) to teach it how to find the patterns that connect a building's characteristics to its energy efficiency.

2. The Training Process

The AI "trains" by looping through historical data (past building records) millions of times:

1. INPUT
EXAMPLES
→
2. MODEL
GUESSES
→
3. CHECK
ANSWER
→
4. ADJUST
WEIGHTS
→
LEARNED
MODEL

During the "Adjust" step, the model changes its internal rules (called "weights") to get closer to the right answer. For example, it learns how much "building age" should matter more than "floor area".

Key Insight: The model only learns from the data you give it. The quality and completeness of your training data directly affects how well the AI performs!
